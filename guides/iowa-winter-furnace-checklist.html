Iowa Winter Furnace Checklist for SE Iowa

Published August 3, 2026 · 5 min read

If you live in Southeast Iowa, you know winter isn't a question of if — it's when. Burlington saw 33 inches of snow last season. Iowa City hit -22°F wind chills. And in towns like Danville, Mount Pleasant, and Fort Madison, a furnace failure in January isn't just an inconvenience — it's a genuine safety risk.

The good news? Most furnace problems are preventable. Here's the pre-winter checklist that SE Iowa homeowners should run through before the first hard freeze.

☐ 1. Replace or Clean Your Air Filter

This is the single easiest thing you can do — and the most commonly skipped. A dirty filter makes your furnace work harder, raises your electric bill, and can cause the heat exchanger to overheat. Check it monthly during heating season. Standard 1-inch fiberglass filters cost under $5 at Menards or Ace Hardware in Burlington. Swap it out now and buy a 3-pack so you don't forget.

☐ 2. Clear Vents and Registers

Walk through every room. Are any vents blocked by furniture, curtains, or carpet? In older SE Iowa homes — especially farmhouses around Danville and West Burlington — registers can get buried under winter coats, boxes, or pet beds. Blocked vents create back-pressure that strains your blower motor. Every vent needs at least 6 inches of clearance.

☐ 3. Test Your Thermostat

Before the cold hits, turn your thermostat to heat and set it 5 degrees above room temperature. Wait 10 minutes. Do you feel warm air coming from the vents? If your thermostat runs on batteries (many programmable models do), replace them now. There's nothing worse than waking up to a 48°F house because the thermostat died overnight.

☐ 4. Inspect the Outdoor Exhaust Vent

High-efficiency furnaces have a PVC exhaust pipe that exits through the side of your house. After a windstorm or heavy snow — common in Iowa City and Burlington — these vents can get blocked by debris, ice, or snow drifts. A blocked exhaust can cause carbon monoxide to back up into your home. Make sure the pipe is clear and pointing away from windows and doors.

☐ 5. Listen for Unusual Noises

Turn the furnace on and listen. Do you hear banging, popping, or screeching? That's not normal. Banging can mean delayed ignition (gas buildup before the burner lights). Screeching is often a worn blower bearing or belt. Either way, call an HVAC pro before the system fails entirely.

☐ 6. Check Carbon Monoxide and Smoke Detectors

Furnace season is CO season. If you don't have a CO detector on every floor of your home, get one today. Burlington Fire Department recommends placing one within 15 feet of each sleeping area. Test all detectors now and replace batteries — even hardwired units have battery backup. This is not optional. It's life safety.

Pro tip from a Danville HVAC tech: "Every January we get 3-4 calls from folks whose furnace won't start. 90% of the time it's either a dirty filter, a tripped limit switch, or the furnace condensate line froze. You can prevent all three with a 15-minute walkthrough in October."

When to Call a Professional

Some things you can DIY. Others you shouldn't. Call an HVAC professional if:

  • The furnace won't ignite or keeps cycling on and off
  • You smell gas (leave the house immediately and call MidAmerican Energy at 800-595-5325)
  • Your pilot light is yellow or orange instead of blue
  • The system is more than 15 years old and hasn't been serviced
  • Your energy bills have jumped significantly without explanation

Get a Pre-Season Tune-Up

Most HVAC companies in SE Iowa offer fall tune-up specials. Burlington HVAC pros typically charge $80-$150 for an annual inspection — and it pays for itself if they catch a cracked heat exchanger or failed capacitor before January. A tune-up should include checking the heat exchanger, cleaning the burners, testing gas pressure, and measuring temperature rise across the system.

Quick Recap

  1. Change your air filter (and stock up)
  2. Clear all vents and registers
  3. Test the thermostat and replace batteries
  4. Check outdoor exhaust for blockages
  5. Listen for unusual furnace noises
  6. Test CO and smoke detectors
  7. Schedule a professional tune-up before November
